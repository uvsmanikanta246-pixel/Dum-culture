The Royal Art of Slow-Dum
Aroma sealed with dough & cooked over slow embers

Legend has it that biryani traveled to India centuries ago along trade routes and royal kitchens, carried by Persian traders and Mughal emperors who cherished the art of slow-cooked rice layered with fragrant spices and meat.

Some say it was Mumtaz Mahal herself who first ordered biryani to be cooked as a hearty, complete meal for soldiers, while others credit the bustling kitchens of the Nizams of Hyderabad for perfecting the dum style of sealed, slow-steamed cooking that we know today.

As the dish spread across regions — from Lucknow's delicate Awadhi biryani to Kolkata's potato-studded version to Hyderabad's fiery, layered masterpiece — each city added its own soul to the recipe, turning a simple rice dish into a symbol of celebration, hospitality, and heritage.
